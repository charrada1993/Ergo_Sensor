Predictive Biomechanics Architecture

The Ergo Sensor AI Engine uses a production-grade machine learning ensemble to deliver real-time ergonomic risk assessment and musculoskeletal disorder (MSD) classification. The system extracts 75 kinematic features—raw joint angles, bilateral asymmetry, rolling statistics, frame-lagged histories, and dynamic accelerations—from 12 body joints captured at 30 FPS.

The pipeline combines a LightGBM Regressor (R²=0.9981) for continuous risk scoring, a multi-class Condition Classifier identifying 18 clinical diagnoses (99.60% accuracy), a Severity Classifier (96.95% accuracy), and 5 per-joint anomaly detectors monitoring Neck, Shoulder, Wrist, Trunk, and Elbow in real time. All hyperparameters are tuned via Optuna with TimeSeriesSplit cross-validation to prevent temporal data leakage.

Model Evaluation Suite

Complete evaluation dashboard generated from the held-out test set (20% temporal split — 4,000 samples). Covers regression accuracy, 18-class condition confusion matrix, severity categorization, feature importance, and all 5 anomaly ROC curves.

LightGBM Regressor predicting continuous ergonomic risk score (0–1). Achieved R²=0.9981 RMSE=0.0079 MAE=0.0056 on the test set. Residuals are tightly centered at zero, confirming minimal systematic bias.

18-class Gradient Boosting Classifier identifying TMS pathologies. Accuracy=99.60% F1 Macro=96.67%. Plots include LogLoss convergence, confusion matrix, per-class ROC-AUC, and Precision-Recall curves.

Severity Classifier categorizing risk into Low / Medium / High bands. Accuracy=96.95% F1 Macro=0.9411. Learning curve and confusion matrix shown below.

5 independent per-joint binary classifiers monitoring Neck Hyperflexion, Shoulder Overextension, Wrist Strain, Trunk Torsion, and Elbow Hyperextension. Average F1 across all joints: ≥ 0.99. ROC-AUC near-perfect on all articular regions.

Training convergence curves for the Risk Regressor (RMSE vs. boosting iteration) and Condition Classifier (LogLoss vs. boosting iteration). Both models converged stably with no overfitting detected.

SHAP TreeExplainer applied to 500 test samples to decode model decision-making. Identifies which kinematic features (rolling means, accelerations, bilateral asymmetry) most strongly drive risk predictions and condition diagnoses.
